Core Formula:
Area = π × r²

Circle to Rectangle

Proof Method 1

Slicing a circle into equal sectors and interlocking top and bottom slices converts the circle into a parallelogram. As the number of slices increases to infinity, the shape becomes a perfect rectangle!

The Mathematical Proof

  • Full Circumference (C): 2πr
  • Rectangle Base (half C): b = πr
  • Rectangle Height: h = r
  • Area = Base × Height = (πr) × r = πr²

Unrolling Nested Rings

Proof Method 2

Imagine a circle made of concentric rings (like onion layers). Cutting along a radius and unrolling all rings horizontally stacks them to form a right-angled triangle!

Triangle Proof Logic

  • Triangle Base (Outer Ring): 2πr
  • Triangle Height (Radius): r
  • Area = ½ × Base × Height = ½ × (2πr) × r = πr²

Why ~3.14 Squares?

Intuition

What does r² actually mean? A square with side length r has an area of r². Four of these squares enclose the entire circle with a bounding box area of 4r².

Trimming off the 4 corner overflow regions leaves a circle area of exactly π ≈ 3.14159 full r² squares!

Bounding Box (4 Squares): 4.00 r²
Circle Area (π Squares): ≈ 3.14 r²
Circle / Bounding Box Ratio: π / 4 ≈ 78.54%

Key Insight

π is fundamentally the ratio of a circle's area to the area of a square constructed on its radius (r × r).

Polygon Approximation

Archimedes

Archimedes approximated circle area by squeezing the circle between an inscribed polygon (inside) and a circumscribed polygon (outside).

Polygon Sides (N): 6
Inscribed Area: 2.598 r²
Exact Circle Area: 3.142 r²
Circumscribed Area: 3.464 r²

Limit Principle

As the number of sides $N \to \infty$, both the inner and outer polygon areas converge precisely to πr²!
